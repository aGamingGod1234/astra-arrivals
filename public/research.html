ASTRA / RESEARCH NOTES

The universe has
more than one entrance.

Nine cinematic families drawn from NASA, ESA and ESO references. The display compresses these ideas into a short sequence that always returns to the number 6.

Colours, timing and the particle responses are artistic interpretations. This is not a physics simulation. Sixfall's digit-shaped comets are deliberately playful. Lensing bends light in the reference; the display translates that into a distortion of the numeral.

01

Supernova

A massive star explodes and throws out an expanding cloud of debris. A shock front travels into its surroundings.

In the display: A star swells, erupts and sends an expanding shock front through the 6. Particles move only when the wave reaches them.

02

Sixfall

A fragmenting comet can separate into several nuclei, each surrounded by its own envelope of gas and dust.

In the display: A fragmented comet becomes a procession of luminous 6s. Their trails carve separate channels through the numeral.

ESA reference ↗
03

Tidal disruption

A star passing close to a black hole can be stretched into a stream that feeds an accretion disc.

In the display: A dark centre opens inside an accretion disc. The 6 stretches into a long stream and spirals inward before returning.

NASA reference ↗
04

Kilonova

Two neutron stars spiral together and merge. Their collision can produce gravitational waves and a kilonova.

In the display: Two bright stars orbit closer, merge and release expanding shells. The 6 ripples, disperses and gathers again.

ESA reference ↗
05

Pulsar

A rotating neutron star emits beams that sweep past an observer like a lighthouse.

In the display: Twin lighthouse beams sweep through space. Each pass lifts a narrow fan of particles out of the 6.

NASA reference ↗
06

Relativistic jet

Material near a black hole can launch narrow, fast jets into the surrounding space.

In the display: A compact source launches a narrow jet. It cuts through the 6, stretches a stream of particles downstream and leaves them to settle.

NASA reference ↗
07

Einstein ring

Gravity can bend the light from a background source into magnified arcs or a ring.

In the display: A passing gravitational lens bends starlight into arcs. The particles follow a warped ring, then the distortion passes.

ESO reference ↗
08

Stellar winds

The colliding winds of a binary star can produce dust shells. Webb observed a series around Wolf-Rayet 140.

In the display: A binary star blows nested, turning dust shells. They comb through the 6 and roll its particles into a spiral.

ESA reference ↗
09

Butterfly nebula

A dying star can expel material in two opposed lobes, creating a butterfly-like nebula.

In the display: Two lobes of starlight unfold from a stellar core. The 6 opens into wing-like streams, then closes back into its original shape.
